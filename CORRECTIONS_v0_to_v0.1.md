# Corrections, version 0 to version 0.1

Version 0.1, 13 August 2026. What changed since the deposit of 12 July 2026, and what was
wrong in it. A scorecard that grades others on sourcing discipline carries a higher duty
here, so the errors are recorded rather than absorbed into a refresh.

AI disclosure: the research is the author's; this text was drafted with AI assistance and
reviewed by the author. The model, and the conflict it creates, are named in the Conflict
of interest and scope section.

## Wrong at the date of deposit

**The version 0 search was incomplete.** Of the three claims added here, two were public
before version 0 was published: OpenAI's "instrumental in creating itself", five months before,
and the Anthropic Institute's figure of more than 80 per cent of merged code, five weeks
before. Version 0 verified each claim it held against a primary but never ran a dated sweep
for claims it did not already hold. Both misses ran in the same direction: they made the
set look less checkable than it was.

**The research-loop count was reported as empty and was not.** Version 0 stated that none
of its claims isolated the research-loop subset. On the held evidence three of the twelve
do, and one of the three was sitting in an April 2025 transcript the whole time. That
column was the piece's bridge to Layer 2, so the error touched its central claim.

## What is new rather than corrected

**Every claim is anchored to a held primary.** Version 0 had no source register and hashed
nothing; its claims file recorded a source as free text. When anchoring was introduced here,
seven of the twelve rows turned out to rest on secondary reporting of the original statement
until the primaries were fetched. Each of the twelve now cites a document the author holds
and has hashed, listed in `source_register.csv`, and `reproduce_v0.1.py` fails if a claim
cites a primary the register does not hold. Reading those primaries is what produced the
next entry.

**Four claims read narrower in their source than in circulation.** Salesforce's figure
changes meaning inside the interview it comes from, moving from a productivity level to a
share of work across three turns. Microsoft's is scoped to some projects rather than all
repositories. The OpenAI conference claim is about the listener's codebase rather than
OpenAI's. Amodei's prediction carries a qualifier that is usually dropped.

**Timeline claims enter Layer 2.** A self-declared arrival date is the same kind of object
as a self-declared threshold. Three rows are added and none carries a definition of what
would count as arrival.

**The register gains an independent cross-lab instrument**, METR's entity-based pilot of
February to April 2026. Version 0 stated that no such instrument existed, which was correct
for its evidence at the time and is superseded rather than withdrawn.

**The Meta row moved three times in one day** before settling. It is recorded as isolating
the research loop, on the reading that "because" joins the half-the-development line to the
preceding clause about an AI and machine learning engineer for Llama. A reader taking "the
development" as generic would score that row otherwise, and both readings are kept in the
claims file.

## Conflict of interest and scope

The research design, method, sourcing decisions and analytical judgements are
the author's. Models from Anthropic and Google assisted with data retrieval, calculation,
literature search and drafting, so this text was artificially generated and was reviewed by
the author before publication. An OpenAI model assisted with the pre-publication red-team
review. The assisting model is not always a neutral party to the subject matter, and
all three vendors are graded parties: Anthropic holds the highest-scoring row in the set
and is named by Snap as the vendor behind its own figure, Google holds a row of its own,
and OpenAI is a graded party.

Guarantee: every measured claim is traced to the primary document named in the
Verification note, and reproduce_v0.1.py regenerates every table from claims_v0.1.csv and
triggers_v0.1.csv. Modelled or estimated inputs are identified as such in the Verification
note rather than presented as measurements. A reader can check this without trusting
either party.

What the author cannot guarantee: a language model's output can be wrong in ways
that survive review. In prose the error is locally plausible and consistent in
tone with what surrounds it; in code it simply runs, and a wrong constant or a
mis-set filter still returns a clean number. Several methods have been deployed to
mitigate this, including explicit instructions, internal red-teaming and
cross-lab blindspot checks, but the author does not claim the review is
exhaustive. Corrections are logged against the DOI when surfaced.

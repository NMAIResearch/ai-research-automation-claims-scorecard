# Conflict of interest, v0.1

Replaces the v0 statement in `README.md` and `SOURCES.md`. Applied on mint, not before.

## The disclosure

This scorecard grades public claims made by AI vendors and adopters. Two of the graded parties
supply tools used to produce it.

**Anthropic** holds the single highest-scoring row in the set, is the most vocal party on recursive
self-improvement, is named by Snap as the vendor behind its own figure, and makes the model that
drafted this text.

**Google** holds the Google row, 75% of new code approved by engineers, and makes the model used
to review the draft (Gemini 3.6, 13 August 2026).

No row was softened, and none was sharpened. The evidence for each is held locally and
hashed, so a reader can check the graded text against the primary without trusting either party or
the author.

## What the assistants did and did not do

The research question, the method, the sourcing decisions and every analytical judgement are the
author's. The models drafted prose, ran checks the author specified, and retrieved and verified
sources against primaries the author obtained. They are instruments under the author's direction.
They are not co-authors and they hold no view.

## The limitation the author cannot remove

Text drafted by a language model can carry errors that are locally plausible, consistent in tone
with the surrounding prose, and therefore able to survive review. Several methods have been
deployed to reduce this, including explicit instructions and internal red-teaming, cross-lab blindspots checks, but the author
does not claim the review is
exhaustive. Corrections are logged against the DOI when surfaced, and `CHANGELOG.md` carries them
in full, including corrections to the published v0.
